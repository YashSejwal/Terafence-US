Case study

Water Facility Secure Syslog Data Telemetry to SOC

How a regional water association meets national SOC reporting rules with air-gap security, low cost and zero disruption

CUSTOMERA regional water association
SOLUTIONTerafence Syslog+
USE CASERegulatory telemetry to a national SOC

1The Problem

Threats to critical infrastructure have been recognized for a long time. A cyberattack on national infrastructure doesn't only put economic security and physical assets at risk; it can also affect public safety and health.

Water and wastewater facilities are a familiar target. They are usually municipally owned and spread across many sites. Most have few IT staff, no dedicated cyber security experts and little budget beyond day-to-day operations. That combination makes cyber resilience both urgent and hard to achieve.

2The Challenge

Regulation requires water facilities to send equipment status data to a dedicated Security Operations Center (SOC) run by the Ministry of Energy. The SOC monitors critical infrastructure at state level so it can detect and stop an attack if and when one happens.

A major water facility needed to send all of its facility telemetry to the Ministry of Energy SOC. It faced three obstacles. Its operations were spread across many fragmented sites. It had little capital to invest in a cyber solution. And connecting its network to an outside SOC would create a new link that could itself be exploited.

The facility needed a solution that met the regulation, protected its network and fit its budget and operations.

3The Solution

The chosen solution had low impact on both cost and operations. The facility's main syslog server data is forwarded to the Ministry of Energy SOC through a Terafence Syslog+ unit, which provides air-gap-level security between the two sites.

  • Syslog data flows from the facility to the SOC in one direction only
  • Air-gap-level separation between the facility network and the SOC
  • No change to the existing network topology
  • No disconnection of the working environment during installation

Because Syslog+ needed no topological change and no downtime, the facility could meet its obligation without disrupting operations.

4The Impact

The facility now sends the telemetry the regulator requires, with a level of security that matches the importance of the infrastructure it runs.

For resource-constrained utilities facing new reporting mandates, Terafence offers a fast, affordable route to compliance and stronger cyber resilience.

  • Regulatory compliance.

    The Ministry of Energy SOC receives the facility's syslog telemetry continuously.

  • Network protected.

    Air-gap-level security ensures the SOC link cannot be used to reach the facility.

  • Low cost, low impact.

    A practical choice for a utility with limited capital and few IT staff.

  • Zero disruption.

    Installed without changing the topology or taking systems offline.

  • Room to grow.

    The association intends to examine further Terafence installations.
