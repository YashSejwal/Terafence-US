Why now

Take Controllers Off the Internet — Then Solve What Put Them There

Nobody wires a PLC to a cellular modem for fun; somebody needed tank levels, feeder status, or state compliance logs at 2 a.m. without driving forty miles. Yet in July 2026 (FBI & EPA Alert I-073026-PSA), attackers targeted 100+ internet-exposed US water and wastewater systems — changing PLC passwords, locking out operators, and causing pressure loss and tank overflows echoing earlier incidents at two US municipal water utilities.

Removing public internet exposure is step one. Replacing bidirectional reporting links with hardware one-way transfer is step two — supporting CISA CPG 2.0 Goal 3.I (data diodes for OT enclaves), SDWA §1433 (AWIA) resilience updates due December 31, 2026, NERC CIP-003-9, and joint NCSC/CISA OT Connectivity Principles (January 2026).

How it works

Hardware Isolation Sized for Municipal & Utility Budgets

Legacy multi-server diodes priced regional utilities out of physical segmentation. Terafence combines Side A and Side B proxy computers with a direction-locked FPGA core (no CPU, no OS, no IP or MAC address on the data path) inside one compact unit — delivering hardware isolation at a fraction of legacy diode TCO, with nothing installed on existing PLCs, RTUs, or SCADA hosts.
