Executive summary

Critical infrastructure is now a standing target. The question is no longer whether a network will be probed, but whether a probe can ever become a path.

In modern industrial and critical infrastructure environments, cyber threats are constant and evolving. Any system — directly or indirectly — can become a point of vulnerability. Power grids, substations, energy plants, dams and industrial control systems are increasingly targeted by ransomware, intrusion attempts and data exfiltration campaigns.

To address this, the unidirectional data diode provides a hardware-enforced, air-gapped solution that ensures secure, one-way data transfer between isolated networks — without exposing internal systems to any form of return communication or logical interaction.

Unlike firewalls or proxies that rely on configurable software, the diode operates through fixed, FPGA-driven logic. It allows only authorized protocols and file types while blocking all others at the hardware level — delivering deterministic, tamper-proof data flow with zero exposure.

“Data leaves. Nothing returns. The return path is not blocked by a rule that can be changed — it does not physically exist.”

Introduction

The silent security threat in isolation: the very gap that protects the network is what makes it hard to patch.

Critical infrastructure, industrial control systems, defense facilities and government institutions rely on air-gapped networks as their final line of defense. These isolated environments ensure maximum security by minimizing external exposure. However, that isolation turns into a vulnerability when it comes to distributing software and patches.

Disconnected from the internet, these environments cannot reach centralized update servers or cloud tooling. Distributing even a simple update or critical security patch becomes a complex, manual and often insecure process.

Why secure patch distribution is critical

Unpatched or outdated systems are among the most exploited vulnerabilities in the threat landscape. Even air-gapped environments have no immunity from insider threats, USB-borne malware or stale and insecure dependencies.

The compliance dimension

Frameworks such as IEC 62443 and NIST SP 800-82, along with national cyber regulations, generally require evidence that secure update processes are in place — even for systems that are not connected to the internet. Traditional USB-based methods cannot produce that evidence.

Challenges

Traditional software and patch distribution for air-gapped networks is slow, blind and unauditable.

  • Manual transfers via removable media, which are slow, insecure and error-prone.
  • No visibility into which systems have received which updates.
  • No centralized control, leading to configuration drift and inconsistent environments.
  • Difficulty maintaining audit logs or update trails for compliance.

Even the act of physically moving files across zones can create an opening for malware if it is not strictly controlled and monitored.

“The gap is the control. Any process that requires a human to carry media across it has already compromised the control.”

Why Terafence

Controlled, unidirectional transfer of software packages from a trusted environment into the air-gapped zone — without ever breaking the gap.

Terafence enables one-way data transfer through hardware-enforced and software-hardened mechanisms: no backflow or reverse communication from the air-gapped network, uninterrupted segmentation of trusted and untrusted zones, and protocol-independent transfer supporting a wide range of data formats and update packages.
