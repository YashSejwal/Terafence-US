Executive summary

Critical infrastructure is now a standing target. The question is no longer whether a network will be probed, but whether a probe can ever become a path.

In modern industrial and critical infrastructure environments, cyber threats are constant and evolving. Any system — directly or indirectly — can become a point of vulnerability. Power grids, substations, energy plants, dams and industrial control systems are increasingly targeted by ransomware, intrusion attempts and data exfiltration campaigns.

To address this, the unidirectional data diode provides a hardware-enforced, air-gapped solution that ensures secure, one-way data transfer between isolated networks — without exposing internal systems to any form of return communication or logical interaction.

Unlike firewalls or proxies that rely on configurable software, the diode operates through fixed, FPGA-driven logic. It allows only authorized protocols and file types while blocking all others at the hardware level — delivering deterministic, tamper-proof data flow with zero exposure.

“Data leaves. Nothing returns. The return path is not blocked by a rule that can be changed — it does not physically exist.”

Introduction

Every connected camera is an addressable computer, mounted where the public can reach its cable.

IP-based CCTV surveillance systems form the nerve center of city-wide, enterprise and critical infrastructure security. However, their connectivity carries substantial risk that, if overlooked, compromises not just visual intelligence but entire centralized control networks.

The real problem

While digitization promises scalability and centralized control, it has also introduced a gaping vulnerability — every connected camera becomes an open window. When hundreds of IP cameras are networked across cities, metros, airports and ports, the risk of one device being breached becomes statistically inevitable.

If even a single outdoor camera is tampered with — or hijacked via its Ethernet cable — it becomes a tunnel into the heart of secure networks such as the Secure City Command & Control Center. Such an intrusion typically goes unnoticed until substantial damage is done.

Scenario — the invisible breach

Picture hundreds of IP cameras streaming to a city's central command center, mounted on poles, buildings and bridges. Now picture one of them compromised. The attacker does not need to breach the data center — only physical access to a camera's network cable. From there they gain network-level access, bypass firewall layers and reach critical internal systems.

To the monitoring team, the breach looks like nothing more than an offline camera while malicious activity runs unnoticed.

Challenges

The shift from analog to digital brought scale and centralized control — and a new class of exposure.

Every networked camera is a potential entry point. As hundreds of IP cameras are connected across cities, airports and other vital infrastructure, the security risk grows exponentially. These systems are now a prime target, and a single compromised camera can be enough to bring down an entire secure network.

  • Vast attack surface. Every camera is an addressable node on the network.
  • Physical vulnerability. Cameras sit in publicly reachable positions, cable and all.
  • Gateway to the core. One camera can become a route into the command center.
  • Stealthy intrusions. A breach can present simply as a camera gone offline.

Applications and use cases

  • Smart cities. Command-center connectivity without exposure.
  • Airports and ports. Border surveillance and crowd monitoring.
  • Metro networks. Trackside and platform security feeds.
  • Law enforcement. Evidence streaming without network risk.
  • Military zones. Prevent compromise of high-stake visuals.
  • Power grids and refineries. Isolated yet live-streamed feeds.

Why Terafence

A plug-and-play unidirectional gateway that enforces absolute isolation.

Terafence enables one-way video feed transfer through the data diode. There is no backflow or reverse communication from the command network to the cameras, and segmentation between the secure control network and the public-facing camera network is never interrupted. We do not inspect or alter files — preserving integrity so they arrive untouched and ready for your video management system.

Footage flows out to monitoring systems; any incoming signal is blocked completely — no commands, no controls, no access path back into the camera network. With no return channel, even a compromised camera gives a threat actor nowhere to pivot.

Case study — a tier-1 city's metro surveillance network

A tier-1 metro city integrated 450+ IP cameras into its unified command center. During a red-team audit, testers exploited an outdoor trackside camera to inject a backdoor into the camera switch. With no unidirectional gateway in place they accessed archived footage, manipulated timestamps, and demonstrated how the breach could allow operational disruption or evidence tampering.

After deploying the unidirectional secure gateway, the video feeds remained intact while the attack surface to the central network was eliminated entirely.

Conclusion

CCTV systems are no longer passive observers — they are high-value network assets vulnerable to infiltration. The unidirectional gateway ensures that while your command center receives feeds seamlessly, no digital route exists back into the system.
