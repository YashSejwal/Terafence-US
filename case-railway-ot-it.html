Securing Railways' OT-IT Convergence with Terafence Data Diodes

How one of the world's largest railway networks streams real-time data from nine OT systems to IT, with hardware-enforced protection against inbound attacks

1The Problem

A major operator in Asia's railways, the fourth-largest network in the world, is going through rapid digital transformation. The network carries more than 23 million passengers and 3 million tons of freight every day across 67,000 route kilometres.

It depends on a wide range of operational technology (OT): power and telecom SCADA, fare collection, passenger information displays, platform screen doors, building management and more. These systems cannot fail. A compromise could halt trains, paralyse stations or put passengers at risk. Transport is now a prime target, with ransomware disrupting German rail in 2017 and an attack halting Iran's railways in 2021.

2The Challenge

The railway needed its OT data in IT systems for energy billing, predictive maintenance, asset management, passenger-flow planning and network monitoring. But the connection that carries that data out can also let attackers in. Once an IT-OT session exists, traffic can flow both ways.

Firewalls couldn't close that gap. They rely on software rules that can be misconfigured, bypassed by zero-day exploits or changed by a malicious insider. They have IP and MAC addresses that attackers can target. And even a "one-way" firewall rule still allows some return traffic.

The operator also needed to meet IEC 62443 requirements for critical-sector OT security. For a network of this importance, even a 0.1% chance of an OT breach was unacceptable.

3The Solution

The operator deployed 17 Terafence TFG-1URP Unidirectional Secure Gateways, built on Terafence's FPGA-based data diode. Each of the nine OT systems has its own diode, so no two OT networks are connected. Data converges on a Central Interface Server and then passes through the IT firewall to analytics and cloud applications.

  • Physically one-way transfer, with no sessions, acknowledgements or handshakes
  • No OS, CPU, IP or MAC address, so the diode is invisible to scans and probes
  • Near-1 Gbps throughput with near-zero latency
  • Supports Modbus, SNMP, HTTPS APIs and SFTP in one unified design
  • IEC 62443-4-2 SL2 certified, with independent penetration testing

4The Impact

The railway now has real-time OT data for analytics and full isolation of its OT systems, two goals that used to be thought incompatible.

The project sets a benchmark for transport cybersecurity: with hardware-enforced isolation, air-gapped protection and digital transformation can work together.

  • Mission-critical systems protected.

    Power, passenger services, telecom, safety and asset-health systems are all air-gapped from IT-originating threats.

  • Real-time intelligence.

    Uses include energy billing, early detection of wheel-bearing wear, telecom tower alarms and live escalator fault alerts.

  • Resilient by design.

    Even if the IT firewall or aggregation server is breached, OT stays physically unreachable.

  • Compliance achieved.

    Aligned with IEC 62443 and national expectations for critical infrastructure.
