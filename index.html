The Terafence approach

One-way gateways let data leave the protected network while blocking reverse network access in hardware.

How it works

Data out.
Nothing back in.

Inside a Terafence one-way gateway, data crosses a hardware-enforced boundary. The reverse path is absent from the circuit, not denied by a rule.

Why FPGA hardware

Protection built into the hardware.

The enforcing path has no addressable presence and no software patch cycle.

Conceptual illustration of the enforcing core.

Discuss the technology
01

No addressable presence

No IP, MAC, CPU or memory on the enforcing path. Nothing on that path to scan or enumerate.

02

Hardware-enforced logic

Hardware behaviour, not firmware policy. No software patch cycle on the enforcing path.

03

Deterministic performance

1 / 10 Gbps

Throughput options vary by model, supporting real-time telemetry and streaming data.

04

Transparent insertion

Fits the existing topology with minimal re-addressing and no agents on protected assets.

Designed to support IEC 62443-4-2 SL2MIL-STD-810FUL / IEC 62368-1FCC Part 15 B Certifications and compliance

See model-specific documentation for certification scope. Enforcing-path claims describe the one-way path, not the complete appliance.

Prove it on your site

A signed-off pilot in under two weeks.

One TFG-121 on a live site, carrying real traffic to your SIEM, HMI or file server. Tested against Terafence's written acceptance procedure, not a lab demo.

  • Data arrives complete and uncorrupted, at the agreed throughput.
  • No reverse TCP session can be opened from the receiving side.
  • The core does not reply to ping, traceroute or ARP.
  • Both parties sign the acceptance before you expand.
