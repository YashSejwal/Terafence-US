Case study

Engine/Turbine Secure Data Telemetry via Modbus

How a leading chemical manufacturer gave its equipment vendor real-time analytics without exposing its OT network to the internet

INDUSTRYChemical manufacturing
SOLUTIONTerafence Modbus+
USE CASEVendor predictive maintenance

1The Problem

Smart manufacturing runs on data. Automation, machine learning and big data analytics turn equipment readings into better plant KPIs, fewer unplanned stoppages and proactive maintenance.

More and more, that data has to leave the plant. Equipment vendors need it to respond faster when something fails and to deliver services such as analytics, asset-life extension and better BOM control. Regulators may require it too. But every outbound connection is also a potential way in, and on an operational technology (OT) network that controls physical processes, a single exposed link can turn a maintenance convenience into a safety and business risk.

2The Challenge

A prominent chemical company needed to connect an engine/turbine to the internet so the vendor could deliver real-time analytics, including asset tracking, predictive maintenance and continuous monitoring of the machine's life and health status.

The original design connected the device's PLC to a gateway, which sent the data straight to the vendor's cloud. It worked, but it created a direct bridge between the plant's OT network and the world wide web. If the gateway or the cloud path were ever compromised, an attacker would have a route back to the PLC controlling critical equipment.

The company needed to give its vendor every data point it required while guaranteeing that nothing could travel back the other way.

3The Solution

Terafence proposed a simple change to the topology: place a Terafence Modbus+ unit between the OT network and the IT network. Modbus+ provides air-gap-level separation, so Modbus data flows outward in one direction only, to the vendor's gateway, and there is no path back into the OT network.

  • One-way Modbus transmission from the PLC to the vendor's gateway
  • Air-gap-level isolation between OT and IT networks
  • Existing gateway and vendor cloud service left unchanged
  • The PLC no longer faces the internet, directly or indirectly

Because the vendor's gateway and cloud platform stay exactly as before, the vendor keeps receiving the data its analytics depend on. The difference is that the connection can no longer be used to reach the plant.

4The Impact

The chemical company now benefits from vendor-driven predictive maintenance without accepting the cyber risk that usually comes with it.

Secure connectivity no longer means choosing between data and safety. With Terafence, industrial operators can share what their partners need and keep their critical systems sealed off from outside threats.

  • OT network protected.

    The bridge between the plant floor and the internet is gone. No inbound traffic can reach the PLC.

  • Real-time analytics preserved.

    Asset tracking, health monitoring and predictive maintenance continue without interruption.

  • Less unplanned downtime.

    The vendor can spot problems early and respond faster, which helps protect plant KPIs.

  • Minimal disruption.

    Security was added by inserting one device, not by redesigning the network.

  • Ready for what's next.

    The same one-way approach can carry data to energy or environment ministries if regulation requires it.
