A Managed Security Provider: Feeding a Cloud SOC Safely

How a managed security provider streams device telemetry from protected customer sites to its cloud SOC, with no route back into those sites

CUSTOMERA managed security provider
SECTORManaged security / SOC
DATA FLOWSite → cloud SOC, one-way

1The Problem

Managed Security Operations Centres (SOCs) have become a core part of how organisations defend themselves. Instead of building an in-house team, a business can rely on a provider's analysts and engineers to watch its environment around the clock, spot suspicious activity and respond quickly.

A SOC is only as good as the data it receives. Device status, health data and system messages have to flow continuously from every monitored site. That creates a paradox. The connection that lets defenders see into a network is also a connection that could let attackers in.

2The Challenge

The provider runs a private-sector SOC that monitors protected customer sites from a cloud-based platform. Its SOC engineers and analysts, SIEM engineers, and PIM and DAM engineers depend on a constant stream of device and system telemetry from those sites.

Every link from a customer site to the cloud is a potential way back into that customer's network. If the SOC platform, a cloud service or a credential on the monitoring side were ever compromised, a two-way connection could become the attacker's route into the very sites the SOC is meant to protect.

The provider needed to give its teams full, continuous visibility while ensuring the monitoring link itself could never become an attack vector.

3The Solution

Terafence hardware was placed at the protected sites to forward device status and SYSLOG-type messages to the provider's cloud SOC in one direction only. Data is collected on-premise and sent outward continuously. No session started from the SOC side can reach site assets.

  • Continuous upload of device status, health data and system messages
  • Hardware-enforced one-way flow from each site to the cloud SOC
  • No inbound path from the cloud or the internet into customer sites
  • Fits SYSLOG-based SOC and SIEM workflows

Because protection is enforced in hardware, it doesn't depend on firewall rules, patch levels or configuration choices that could change over time.

4The Impact

The provider delivers its managed SOC service with the visibility its teams need and the isolation its customers expect.

Monitoring services get the data they need. Attackers get no route back through the monitoring link.

  • Full SOC visibility.

    Analysts and SIEM, PIM and DAM engineers receive the telemetry they need, continuously.

  • Monitored sites stay isolated.

    Nothing on the SOC or cloud side can open a session into a customer's network.

  • A safer managed service.

    Customers can adopt cloud SOC monitoring without accepting new inbound risk.

  • Trust built in.

    The provider can show customers that the monitoring link carries data out and nothing back in.
